# For You Design

## Summary

For You is a short ranked digest of posts, shown during the full-app
windows of conversation mode. It takes posts from the people the user
follows on Mastodon and Bluesky, from three Bluesky topic feeds, and from
an "Always show" list. It does not show what is popular on either network.
The ranking uses fixed rules, not a learned model and not an LLM. The rules
come from the For You lab (`/_foryou-lab`), where the user judged each
rule's picks blind.

The digest ends. It shows the posts that arrived since the user last
finished a digest, then stops.

## Definition of Done

- During a full-app window, a For You page shows one digest. The digest
  holds every post from the "Always show" list first, then the blended
  rule picks, then two random posts.
- The digest covers the posts from 30 minutes before the user last
  reached the end of a digest, at most 24 hours back. It leaves out the
  posts that an earlier digest showed.
- Last read, the posts already shown and the verdicts are in browser
  storage behind one module, which a later change moves to a service on
  deepthought.
- Each post shows one line that tells why it is there, for example
  "Shared by 4 people you follow" or "Housing feed, 395 likes".
- Each post has "Not for me", with the lab's one-tap reasons: mute or block
  the author, mute a hashtag, mute selected text, or mute a typed word.
  Blocking needs no confirmation.
- The page tells how many posts the filters removed, by filter.
- Likes, boosts and replies made from the digest count as "interesting"
  for the rule that picked the post. "Not for me" counts against it.
- The lab stays available for tuning, and reads the same rules.
- Unit tests cover the blend, the window and the reason line.

## Glossary

- **Digest**: One finite, ranked list of posts.
- **Rule**: A function that scores a candidate post. The rules are in
  `RULES` in `src/utils/foryou-lab.js`.
- **Stranger**: An author the user does not follow on that network.
- **"Always show" list**: A list, on either network, with the title
  "Always show". Every post by its members goes into the digest.
- **Last read**: The time at which the user last reached the end of a
  digest.

## Evidence from the lab

The user judged the lab's picks as "Interesting" or "Not for me" without
knowing which rule chose each post. Each rule picks 12 posts. A post that
two rules chose counts for both.

| Rule | 2026-10-23 | 2026-11-05 |
|---|---|---|
| relative (engagement ÷ √followers) | 11/12 | 9/9 |
| raw (engagement) | 11/12 | 7/8 |
| topics (keyword match) | 12/12 | 9/9 |
| shared (boosted or quoted by follows) | 10/12 | 8/8 |
| affinity (authors the user interacts with) | 11/12 | 5/5 |
| quiet (authors who post least) | 8/12 | 3/3 |
| feeds (strangers in topic feeds) | 5/12 | 6/9 |
| random | 8/12 | 0/0 |

The 2026-11-05 run is a partial recovery. The user closed the first run
of that day before exporting it. Only the posts that the reopened lab
picked again kept their verdicts. About the closed run, the user said that
almost every post was interesting or funny. That statement is an
observation, not a count.

The two runs differ: between them, the lab added a language filter, muted
words in link cards, alt text and quoted posts, a 20-like minimum for
strangers, and the removal of replies. So the table does not show the
effect of any one change.

What the evidence supports:

- All rules except `feeds` are close to 100%. The digest can blend them
  instead of choosing one.
- From the San Francisco feed, 0 of 8 posts by strangers were judged
  interesting over both runs. From the AI/ML feed, 10 of 12 were. So the
  lab does not let the San Francisco feed bring in strangers.
- With 3 to 12 judged posts per rule, small differences between rules are
  not evidence.

## Design

### Sources

- The home timeline of each logged-in account, fanned out with
  `api({ account })` as in the merged timeline.
- The three topic feeds in `LAB_FEEDS`. Only Housing and AI/ML may bring in
  strangers.
- The "Always show" list on each network, if the user has made one.

### Filters

These apply before ranking, as in `buildPool`:

- Muted and blocked authors and boosters, on both networks.
- Mastodon home filters, and Bluesky muted words. The filter checks muted
  words against the post text, content warning, link card, alt text and
  the quoted post.
- Replies, including later posts of a self-thread.
- The user's own posts and direct posts.
- Posts not in English or Spanish. Posts from `mstdn.tokyocameraclub.com`
  pass, because the photo is the point. If a post in another language
  quotes an English or Spanish post, the digest shows the quoted post
  instead.

### Blend

The digest takes picks from the rules in turn, one at a time, and skips a
post that an earlier rule took. This is team-draft interleaving
([Radlinski, Kurup and Joachims, CIKM 2008](https://doi.org/10.1145/1458082.1458092)).
Each post keeps the name of the rule that took it, so a like or a "Not for
me" in the digest scores that rule. The lab's comparison then continues in
daily use.

Two random posts go in at random positions. They show whether the rules
continue to do better than chance.

The digest holds about 25 posts plus the "Always show" posts. The
per-author cap stays at 2, except for the "Always show" list.

### Window: since last read

The full-app windows are 08:00–09:00, 12:00–13:00 and 21:00–21:30 on
weekdays, and 08:00–09:00 and 21:00–21:30 on weekends
(`FULL_APP_WINDOWS` in `src/utils/conversation-schedule.js`). So the gap
between two readings is at most about 11 hours.

When the user reaches the end of a digest, the app records last read and
the posts that the digest showed. The next digest starts 30 minutes before
last read, and leaves out the posts already shown. If nothing is new, the
page says so and offers the previous digest.

The 30-minute overlap is for post age. Engagement counts grow with time,
so a post from 10 minutes ago has fewer likes than a post from 3 hours ago,
and the `relative`, `raw` and `shared` rules rank it low. The overlap gives
each recent post that the digest did not show a second chance, with more
engagement. The digest does not correct scores for age. A recent post that
the second digest also ranks low is not shown. The user decided on
2026-11-05 to accept this loss.

### Coverage

The app reads only part of the window:

- **Mastodon**: on 2026-11-05 the home timeline ended after exactly 800
  posts, 4 hours back. The server keeps a fixed number of home posts
  (800 on that server), so more pages give no older posts.
- **Bluesky**: on 2026-11-05, 30 pages of 40 posts covered 1 hour. The lab
  reads up to 100 pages. At that rate, 11 hours needs about 330 pages.

The "Always show" list removes this limit for the accounts on the list,
because the timeline of a short list covers days. For the other accounts,
the digest ranks only the posts that the app can read. Posts from earlier
in a long gap, such as overnight, are not shown. The user decided on
2026-11-05 to accept this loss. A service that collects posts all day is
out of scope.

### Storage

Last read, the posts already shown and the "Not for me" verdicts must be
the same on every device. Browser storage is per device, so a small HTTP
service on deepthought keeps them. The user decided this on 2026-11-05. The
service is reachable on the tailnet only, like the app. The "Always show"
list is on the networks' servers, so it needs no storage.

The user also asked to consider a larger role for this service: keeping
the account sessions on the server, in one place, instead of in each
browser. Each browser holds its own copy of the Mastodon tokens and the
Bluesky refresh token. The Bluesky refresh token is single-use, so two
copies can invalidate each other. Several session bugs came from this. That
change needs its own design plan. The For You storage must not prevent it.

Until the service exists, the For You page keeps its state in browser
storage behind one interface, so that the change to the service touches
one module.

## Open questions

1. **Account sessions on the deepthought service.** See Storage. This
   needs its own design plan.
2. **Topic keyword errors.** The topic patterns match words such as
   "residential" (housing) and "AI" in unrelated text. The user decided on
   2026-11-05 not to tune them yet.

## Out of scope

- An LLM ranker.
- A learned model.
- Posts that are popular on either network but not connected to the
  user's follows or topic feeds.
- A For You view in conversation mode.
- A service that collects posts all day to cover the gaps between
  readings.
